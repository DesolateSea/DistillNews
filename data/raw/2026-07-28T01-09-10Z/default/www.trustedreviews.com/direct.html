Samsung Galaxy Watch 9 vs Apple Watch Series 11: The wearables compared

By Jessica GorringeJuly 27, 20265 Mins Read
Share Facebook Twitter Pinterest LinkedIn Tumblr Reddit Telegram Email
Share
Facebook Twitter LinkedIn Pinterest Email

Looking for an easy way to track your health and workouts, but are torn between a Samsung or Apple offering? You’ve come to the right place.

Sure, while the decision between the Samsung Galaxy Watch 9 and the Apple Watch Series 11 will mostly boil down to whether you’re sporting a Samsung or an iPhone, there are a few other differences that are worth keeping in mind. 

While we’re yet to conclusively review either smartwatch, we’ve compared the specs of the Galaxy Watch 9 to the Apple Watch Series 11 and highlighted the main differences between the two below.

Once you’re finished here, check out our Samsung Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 vs Apple Watch Ultra 3 comparison, while our Samsung Galaxy Watch 9 vs Watch 8 guide explains what’s new with this year’s flagship wearable. Finally, our round ups of the best smartwatches and best fitness trackers will help you find your next device.

Price and Availability

The Samsung Galaxy Watch 9 has a starting price of £349 and is currently available for pre-order ahead of its official launch on August 7th.

Advertisement

On the other hand, the Apple Watch Series 11 is available to buy now with an RRP of £369 for the aluminium iteration. This price rises quite substantially up to £649 for the titanium option. 

Galaxy Watch 9 is powered by WearOS while the Apple Watch Series 11 runs on WatchOS

One of the biggest differences between the Galaxy Watch 9 and the Apple Watch Series 11 is with their respective operating systems. While the Galaxy Watch 9 runs on Samsung’s custom One UI that’s based on Google’s WearOS, the Apple Watch 11 uses Apple’s own WatchOS platform.

This means that while the Samsung Galaxy Watch 9 can be paired with most Android phones (although for the best experience, you’re better off pairing it with a Samsung handset), whereas the Apple Watch 11 can only be paired with an iPhone. As we mentioned earlier, this alone will likely be the deciding factor when choosing between the smartwatches.

For a closer look at the differences, visit our dedicated WatchOS vs WearOS guide.

Galaxy Watch 9 runs on Qualcomm’s Snapdragon Wear Elite

Powering the Galaxy Watch 9 is Qualcomm’s latest wearable chip, Snapdragon Wear Elite. Not only does Snapdragon Wear Elite promise to boost performance by up to 35%, but it’s also the world’s first wearable platform that features a dedicated NPU for personal AI. That means the Galaxy Watch 9 can now support hands-free access to Gemini too. 

Advertisement

In comparison, the Apple Watch 11 is powered by Apple’s S10 chip which is the same that powers the Apple Watch Series 10 and Apple Watch SE 3 too. While we’re yet to specifically review the Watch 11, we have spent time with the Apple Watch Series 10 and found it to be a solid performer and able to do everything from measure data and record activity to open Siri without any delay. With this in mind, we’d reasonably expect the Apple Watch 11 to provide a similar performance.

Apple Watch Series 11 provides hypertension notifications

When it comes to health and fitness tracking, generally the Galaxy Watch 9 and Apple Watch 11 are evenly matched. Both are equipped with heart rate, blood pressure and other Vitals monitoring, GPS for accurate location pinning and great sleep tracking tools too, including sleep apnea notifications too. In fact, our Mobile Editor Lewis Painter was notified of his potential sleep apnea with last year’s Galaxy Watch 8. 

However, the Apple Watch Series 11 also benefits from hypertension notifications which should alert users for signs of chronic high blood pressure. 

Apple Watch Series 11 comes in larger sizes

Both the Galaxy Watch 9 and the Apple Watch Series 11 are hailed as being everyday smartwatches that are just the right side of sporty. If you prefer the more rugged, adventurous design then you’d be better off opting for the Ultra alternatives for both the Galaxy Watch and Apple Watch.

Advertisement

While both come in a choice between two sizes, the Apple Watch Series 11’s options are larger than the Galaxy Watch 9. So, while the Galaxy Watch 9 comes as either a 40mm or 44mm size, the Apple Watch 11 is available as a 42mm or 46mm. 

Whether you prefer the larger Apple Watch 11 or the smallest Galaxy Watch 9 will depend on your personal preference. For example, our Editor Max Parker loved the 46mm Apple Watch 10, and found it comfortable and easy to use.

Early Verdict

Deciding between the Samsung Galaxy Watch 9 and the Apple Watch Series 11 will mainly boil down to what smartphone you have. If you’re sporting an Android, or better yet a Samsung Galaxy, then the Galaxy Watch 9 is the one for you. On the other hand if you’re a devout iPhone user, then the Apple Watch Series 11 is your best option.
