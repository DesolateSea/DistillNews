Dry, gritty eyes may not be dry eye syndrome but Sjögren's syndrome

  • 기자명 Kim Kyoung-Won
  • Published 2026.07.27 15:48

SNS 기사보내기

I share this article

Dry, gritty eyes are commonly attributed to dry eye syndrome, but in some cases they may be a sign of Sjögren's syndrome instead.

The likelihood of Sjögren's syndrome is higher in people with rheumatic diseases or in those who experience both dry eyes and dry mouth. That said, dry eye syndrome remains by far the more common cause of eye discomfort.

In a video published on the Hanyang University Hospital YouTube channel, Professor Kim Ji-hong of the hospital's Department of Ophthalmology explained that dry eye syndrome often develops with age as tear production declines. It can also be triggered by environmental factors such as prolonged computer or smartphone use, air conditioning, or contact lens wear.

Certain symptoms may help distinguish Sjögren's syndrome from ordinary dry eye disease.

"In Sjögren's syndrome, the problem is not just mildly dry eyes," Professor Kim said. "The immune system impairs the function of the tear glands and salivary glands, causing severe dryness in both the eyes and the mouth."

Accordingly, patients often experience symptoms beyond simple eye discomfort.

"Many patients describe the sensation as if grains of sand are rolling around in their eyes," Kim said. "They often say it is difficult to keep their eyes open, or that their eyes constantly sting and feel sore."

Professor Kim added that persistent dry mouth accompanying dry eyes should not be overlooked.

"If dry eyes are accompanied by frequent dry mouth, difficulty swallowing food without water, or swelling around the parotid or salivary glands, people should not dismiss the symptoms as ordinary dryness and should consider the possibility of Sjögren's syndrome," he said.
