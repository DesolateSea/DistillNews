          Reduce high blood pressure and associated risks by drinking 1 red drink

          If you have been told you have high blood pressure, you might want to consider enjoying one red drink.

          Comments
          News
          Chanelle Georgina Senior Audience Writer
          10:20, 17 Apr 2025

          High blood pressure, also known as hypertension, can be a serious life-threatening condition. This is because prolonged high blood pressure increases the risk of heart disease, heart attacks, strokes, and vascular dementia.

          ‌

          The NHS confirmed that any blood pressure reading of 140/90mmHg in a medical setting is considered high blood pressure (if you're under 80 years of age).

          ‌

          For a home blood pressure monitor, any reading above 135/85mmHg is considered to be high blood pressure (also if you're under the age of 80).

          ‌

          For those over the age of 80, an at-home reading of 145/85mmHg or more is classed as high blood pressure; it'll be 150/90mmHg or higher in a clinical setting.

          To help lower your blood pressure and reduce the health risks associated with the condition, you might want to incorporate one red drink into your diet.

          READ MORE: Eating two pieces of common fruit each day can 'significantly' lower cholesterol levels
          ‌

          Leading charity British Heart Foundation (BHF) has suggested that "beetroot juice might help lower blood pressure".

          The charity elaborated: "Beetroot juice can be a concentrated source of nitrates, and it’s thought that one of their effects is to lower blood pressure."

          ‌

          Nitrates can also be found in other fruits and vegetables, such as spinach, celery, kale, bananas and strawberries.

          Diet plays a crucial role in managing blood pressure levels; healthy eating is one of the best ways to reduce blood pressure readings.

          ‌

          In addition to fruits and vegetables, eating wholegrains, lean protein, and low-fat dairy is recommended.

          These are the foundations for the DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) diet.

          "[The DASH diet] has been shown to be helpful by numerous studies," the BHF noted, expanding on each component.

          ‌

          Take wholegrains, for example, which are rich in fibre and are "linked to a lower risk of heart and circulatory diseases".

          Article continues below

          Wholegrains

          • Brown rice
          • Wholemeal bread
          • Wholemeal oats

          Lean protein is "helpful in lowering blood pressure" because it's rather filling and so can help prevent somebody from overeating.

          Food sources of lean protein include beans, eggs, fish, turkey, and chicken.
