Dengue can look like a simple viral fever at first: Doctor reveals the warning signs that help tell the difference before complications begin

Aadya Jha / TIMESOFINDIA.COM / Jul 27, 2026, 19:37 IST
Share
AA
Text Size
  • Small
  • Medium
  • Large
Follow us
1/7

Dengue and viral fever often begin with similar symptoms


Monsoon brings relief from the heat, but it also marks the return of mosquito-borne illnesses. Across many Indian homes, a fever often starts the same way: a slight chill, tiredness, body pain, and the hope that a couple of days of rest will solve everything. Sometimes it does.

But sometimes, what looks like an ordinary viral fever turns out to be dengue, a disease that can worsen quickly if ignored.

The challenge is that dengue does not always announce itself dramatically. In the first few days, it can closely resemble many other viral infections. That is why doctors say guessing at home or relying on internet advice can delay the right treatment.


2/7

Dengue is not just another viral fever

Many people use the phrase "viral fever" for almost every fever that lasts a few days. Medically, however, viral fever is not a single disease.

Dr Shilpa Rajendran, Chief of Medical Services & Consultant Intensivist, Even Healthcare, explained, "Dengue is a specific viral infection caused by the dengue virus and spread through the bite of infected Aedes mosquitoes. The infection can range from having no symptoms at all to causing a mild fever, or in some cases, severe complications such as dengue shock syndrome."

She further said, "Viral fever, on the other hand, is a general term used for any fever caused by a virus, such as influenza, COVID-19, or chickenpox."

That distinction matters because the cause, the possible complications, and even the monitoring required can be completely different.

While most viral fevers improve with rest, fluids, and time, dengue requires careful observation because some patients can deteriorate after the fever begins to settle.

3/7

Why the symptoms can fool almost anyone

One of the biggest reasons dengue gets missed is because its early symptoms overlap with several common viral illnesses.

A person may develop:

Sudden high fever
Headache
Fatigue
Muscle aches
Loss of appetite
​
These symptoms alone cannot confirm whether someone has dengue or another viral infection.

However, dengue often begins to show certain clues that doctors pay close attention to.

Dr Rajendran said, "While dengue and other viral fevers can have similar symptoms, dengue often stands out because of severe body aches, intense joint, bone pain and retro orbital pain and a rapid drop in platelet count. Doctors also use blood tests such as the NS1 antigen test and IgM/IgG antibody tests to confirm a dengue infection."

Pain behind the eyes, often called retro-orbital pain, is one symptom that is more commonly associated with dengue than with routine viral fevers. Many patients also describe feeling as though every muscle and bone aches, which is why dengue has historically been nicknamed "breakbone fever."

Still, doctors caution against trying to diagnose dengue based only on symptoms because many illnesses can overlap.

4/7

Why getting the diagnosis right changes everything

Perhaps the biggest misconception is that platelet count alone decides whether someone has dengue. Doctors say this is only one part of the picture.

A confirmed diagnosis helps healthcare teams monitor the patient's hydration status, blood counts, warning signs, and overall clinical condition at the right time.

5/7

The days when fever comes down can actually be the most important

Many families assume that once the fever disappears, recovery has begun. With dengue, that assumption can sometimes be dangerous.

The WHO explains that the critical phase often begins around the time the fever settles, usually after several days of illness. During this period, some patients may develop warning signs that require urgent medical attention.

6/7

What should you do if you develop a fever during dengue season?

There is no need to panic every time a fever appears. But there is every reason to be careful.

While waiting for medical advice:
​
Drink adequate fluids unless advised otherwise by your doctor.
Rest as much as possible.
Do not self-medicate without medical guidance.
Watch carefully for any warning signs, especially if the fever begins to settle.
If your doctor recommends blood tests, get them done at the advised time rather than too early or too late.

Finally, prevention remains the strongest defence. Since dengue spreads through Aedes mosquitoes, removing stagnant water around homes, using mosquito repellents, wearing full-sleeved clothing, and keeping windows protected with screens can significantly reduce the risk of infection.

A fever may look ordinary on day one. But paying attention to the small differences, seeking medical advice early, and avoiding assumptions can make all the difference when dengue is circulating in the community.
