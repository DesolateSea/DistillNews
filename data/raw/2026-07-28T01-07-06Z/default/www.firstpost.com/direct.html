Google confirms Pixel 11 price rise ahead of launch, blames global memory chip crunch

Google has all but confirmed that the upcoming Pixel 11 series will cost more than its predecessors. The company says soaring memory prices and mounting component costs have forced it to adjust pricing, ending years of absorbing supply chain pressures ahead of its Made by Google launch event.

Google Pixel 11 Pro

Google has effectively confirmed that its next-generation Pixel smartphones will launch with higher price tags, citing an unprecedented surge in memory costs that has reshaped the economics of building premium smartphones.

The confirmation comes just weeks before Google’s Made by Google event on 12 August, where the company is expected to unveil the Pixel 11 family. While reports of a potential price increase have circulated for months, Google has now publicly acknowledged that consumers should expect pricing changes, pointing to rising component costs that are affecting the wider technology industry.

Speaking to 9to5Google, Google’s Vice President of Devices and Services, Shakil Barkat, said the company has been grappling with what he described as a “severe, supplier-driven RAM memory crisis.” He suggested that the dramatic rise in memory prices has left manufacturers with little room to continue absorbing higher production costs.

According to Barkat, “there’s never been an increase in memory prices like the world’s going through right now.” He referred to Morgan Stanley figures indicating that the cost of 1GB of RAM has climbed from roughly $2.80 in 2025 to around $12 in 2026âa more than fourfold jump that has significantly increased the cost of producing smartphones.

Google says higher costs can no longer be absorbed

For several product generations, smartphone makers have attempted to shield buyers from fluctuating component prices by absorbing much of the additional expense themselves. Google says that approach is becoming increasingly difficult as suppliers continue raising prices.

Barkat said the company had “shielded our consumers from supply fluctuations for as long as possible,” but added that “economics have fundamentally shifted and we’re not immune to that.” Instead of maintaining existing prices, Google will introduce pricing “adjustments” for the Pixel 11 lineup, with changes that will be implemented dynamically in response to supply conditions.

Despite the expected increase, Google says it intends to keep the Pixel range competitive through trade-in programmes, promotional discounts and bundled subscriptions. Barkat described offers such as Google One as an important part of the company’s strategy to make its flagship devices more affordable, even as manufacturing costs continue to rise.

The comments also reflect a broader challenge facing the consumer electronics industry. Semiconductor shortages may have eased compared with the pandemic years, but manufacturers are now contending with sharp increases in memory pricing, particularly for advanced DRAM used in flagship smartphones and AI-powered devices.
