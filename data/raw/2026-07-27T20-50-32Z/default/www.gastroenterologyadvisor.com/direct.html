Weight Loss, Reduced Ultra-Processed Food Intake Drive Liver Fat Decrease in MASLD

Colby Stong
|
Publish Date July 27, 2026
Weight loss and reduced ultra-processed food intake, not diet type, lead to liver fat improvement in MASLD.

Structured nutritional and lifestyle counseling, not a specific diet, are associated with significant improvement in liver fat content among patients with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), according to a study in JHEP Reports.

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT073043336) at outpatient clinics between February 2022 and September 2024. Eligible participants were aged 18 years and older, had a body mass index (BMI) of 25 kg/m2 or higher and 35 kg/m2 or lower, had a confirmed diagnosis of MASLD, and had no previous exposure to structured nutritional counseling for MASLD. They were randomly assigned 1:1:1 to adhere to the Mediterranean diet (MeD), low-carbohydrate (LCHO) diet, or standard nutritional recommendations (control group).

The primary outcome included changes in controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) after 6 months of diet adherence. All participants received standardized verbal and written nutritional information from trained dieticians in a 90-minute individual counseling session.

The study enrolled 173 patients — 58 were randomized to the LCHO group, 58 to the MeD group, and 57 to the control group. Among the participants, 25 discontinued during the follow-up (8 in the LCHO group, 9 in the MeD group, and 8 in the control group). Mean ages were 49.5 years in the LCHO group (men, 60%), 50.7 years in the MeD group (men, 61%), and 50 years in the control group (men, 65%).

These findings support a pragmatic, patient-centered approach to dietary management in MASLD, prioritizing sustainable weight loss and reduction of UPF over rigid adherence to a specific dietary model.

At 6 months, CAP values decreased significantly within the LCHO group (Δ -19.5 [-48.8 to 8.0], P =.004) and the control group (Δ -23.0 [-54.0 to -1.00]; P <.001), and the decrease in the MeD group (Δ -11.0 [-32.0 to 12.0]; P =.062) was not statistically significant. No significant differences in CAP change were found among the 3 study arms.

The 3 dietary groups had significant within-group decreases in body weight, BMI, waist circumference, and neck circumference at 6 months postintervention, with a similar magnitude of change among the groups. Self-reported physical activity based on the International Physical Activity Questionnaire increased significantly for all groups, with no significant between-group differences. Sitting time was modestly reduced in the 3 groups.

Aspartate aminotransferase, alanine aminotransferase, and gamma-glutamyl transferase decreased significantly in the control group during the intervention period. Lipid profiles had slight changes among groups, but no consistent between-group differences. Each dietary group had a significant decrease in total energy intake during the 6-month intervention, and saturated fatty acid intake was significantly reduced in all groups.

In the structural equation model analysis, decreases in BMI and ultra-processed food (UPF) intake were independently and significantly associated with improvement in CAP. Greater decreases in BMI were associated with larger reductions in CAP (β=14.34; 95% CI, 10.55-18.13), and greater decreases in UPF intake also were correlated with greater improvement in CAP (β=0.64; 95% CI, 0.25-1.03).

Among several limitations, physical activity was evaluated with self-reported questionnaires, and dietary intake was based on food diaries. In addition, the follow-up was 6 months, and all participants received nutritional counselling and repeated clinical assessments, which could have promoted lifestyle changes.

“These findings support a pragmatic, patient-centered approach to dietary management in MASLD, prioritizing sustainable weight loss and reduction of UPF over rigid adherence to a specific dietary model,” the study authors concluded.

References:

Bo S, Armandi A, Castelnuovo G, et al. Impact of weight loss and reduction of ultra-processed foods on liver fat content in MASLD: a randomized controlled trial. JHEP Rep. Published online June 21, 2026. doi:10.1016/j.jhepr.2026.101929
